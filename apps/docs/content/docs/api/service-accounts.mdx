---
title: Service accounts
description: Use non-human FortyOne principals for deployed integrations without sharing a person's token.
---

A service account represents a deployed process rather than a person. Use one
when an integration must continue independently of an employee's account.

The `/api/v1` preview accepts a service-account key for idempotent story
creation. Give that key `stories:write` and restrict it to only the teams the
process owns. The story mutation keeps the service account as the actor; it
does not invent a human reporter or silently borrow the installer's membership.

Workspace, team, and story reads still depend on live human membership in this
preview, and outbound-webhook management requires a current human workspace
administrator. Those operations return `principal_not_supported` for a
service-account key. Use a narrowly scoped PAT for the read and management
operations, or a user-authorized token for the exact
`https://api.fortyone.app/api/v1` OAuth resource, until their non-human policies
are explicitly versioned.

Service accounts are deliberately non-privileged:

- their workspace role can be `guest` or `member`, never `admin`;
- their keys cannot create or manage other service accounts;
- every key has its own scopes, optional team restrictions, expiry, prefix,
  last-used time, and revocation state;
- disabling an account revokes all of its keys atomically.

Workspace administrators create and disable service accounts and manage their
keys. Store each show-once key in the consuming system's secret manager. Prefer
immediate rotation; request a short overlap only when two deployed generations
must coexist during a controlled rollout.

Use different service accounts for systems with different ownership or access
requirements. This produces clearer audit attribution and smaller incident
blast radius than sharing one broad credential.

The runnable external-integration sample uses a PAT for its initial story sync
and a separate `FORTYONE_WRITE_TOKEN` service-account key for story creation.
See the [API quickstart](./quickstart) for the configuration.
