FortyOne works best when each layer answers a different question. You do not
need to use every layer on day one.

| Concept    | The question it answers                  | Usually contains                                    |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Strategy   | Where are we going?                      | An ultimate goal and strategic pillars              |
| Objective  | What outcome are we pursuing?            | Owner, health, dates, and key results               |
| Key result | How will we know it worked?              | A measurable target and current progress            |
| Roadmap    | What are we likely to deliver, and when? | Priorities linked to objectives and work            |
| Feedback   | What needs attention from the team?      | Requests, votes, comments, and status               |
| Team       | Who owns this part of the work?          | Members, workflow, labels, and team settings        |
| Work item  | What needs to happen next?               | Owner, status, priority, estimate, dates, and links |
| Sprint     | What are we committing to now?           | A time-boxed set of team work                       |

## A simple workflow

1. Capture a request, customer signal, field update, or internal need.
2. Connect it to the objective, program, or roadmap priority it supports.
3. Break the decision into owned work with clear estimates.
4. Schedule focused work when timing matters.
5. Use progress and delivery signals to update the roadmap and stakeholders.

The links are useful, but optional. A small team can begin with teams and work
items, then add objectives, feedback, scheduling, and integrations as the need
becomes real.
